1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROJECT PURPOSE
================================================================================
WearItNow is a browser-based Augmented Reality (AR) virtual try-on system that
enables users to upload any 2D photograph of a piece of clothing (t-shirt,
hoodie, formal shirt, or jacket) and instantly visualize it realistically fitted,
illuminated, and deformed to their body in real time (30–60 FPS) through a
standard RGB webcam or on pre-calibrated reference subjects.

Unlike traditional virtual try-on systems that rely on 2D image-to-image
generative models (which suffer from high server compute costs, 5–10 second
delays per frame, and temporal jitter) or rigid 3D assets that fail to adapt to
different body shapes, WearItNow combines:
1. Lightweight, zero-shot Deep Learning backend models for instant automatic
   garment segmentation and classification.
2. Real-time browser-side 3D skeletal tracking and dense anatomical body cages.
3. Advanced Dual-Engine 3D procedural cloth deformation models (SMPL Human Mesh
   Recovery and Landmark Deformer) running completely client-side in WebGL.

3. BACKEND SERVER & AI PROCESSING PIPELINE (PYTHON / FASTAPI)
================================================================================

3.1. Server Launcher & Lifecycle (`run.py`, `server/main.py`)
------------------------------------------------------------
- `run.py`:
  * Detects current Python environment and verifies required packages (FastAPI,
    Uvicorn, PyTorch, Transformers, rembg, OpenCV, Pillow).
  * Automatically binds to `http://localhost:8000` and launches Uvicorn.
  * Serves the frontend static files from `static/` and mounts API routes.
- `server/main.py`:
  * Uses FastAPI's `@asynccontextmanager` lifespan to preload AI models into
    memory at startup, preventing first-request inference delays.
  * Configures CORS headers and enforces cache-busting middleware (`no-cache,
    no-store, must-revalidate`) so updated textures and client scripts refresh
    instantly.
  * Exposes `/api/health` and `/api/process-garment`.

3.2. Zero-Shot Garment Classification (`server/services/classifier.py`)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Technology: OpenAI CLIP (ViT-B/32) loaded via HuggingFace `transformers`.
- Prompt Engineering: Employs zero-shot classification with curated natural
  language text queries:
  * T-Shirt: "a plain t-shirt, short-sleeve crewneck shirt, casual tee"
  * Hoodie: "a hooded sweatshirt, hoodie with hood and drawstrings"
  * Formal Shirt: "a button-down dress shirt with collar, formal collared shirt"
  * Jacket: "a zip-up jacket, winter coat, denim jacket, blazer"
- Cosine Similarity: Computes normalized dot product between image embeddings
  and text embeddings to produce softmax confidence scores.
- CV Fallback: If PyTorch or CLIP fails, an OpenCV computer vision fallback
  evaluates garment aspect ratios (height / width), collar contour concave dips,
  and sleeve protrusions.

3.3. Deep Garment Segmentation (`server/services/segmenter.py`)
---------------------------------------------------------------
- Technology: `rembg` powered by the U2-Net Portable ONNX deep neural network
  (~4.7 MB).
- Operation: Extracts salient foreground garments from complex e-commerce, flat-
  lay, or mannequin backgrounds without requiring green screens.
- Fallback: OpenCV GrabCut algorithm with rectangular outer border initialization
  and Gaussian bilateral smoothing to isolate clothing masks when offline.

3.4. Texture Extraction & Optimization (`server/services/texture_processor.py`)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Bounding Box Normalization: Trims empty transparent margins around segmented
  garments.
- Aspect Ratio Alignment: Rescales and centers textures into normalized square
  or proportional power-of-two RGBA maps.
- Anti-Aliasing: Applies morphological alpha erosion along cut edges to remove
  halo artifacts from background colors.

4. FRONTEND AR TRACKING & COMPUTER VISION SUBSYSTEM (JAVASCRIPT)
================================================================================

4.1. MediaPipe BlazePose 33-Landmark 3D Tracking (`static/js/tracker.js`)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Technology: Google MediaPipe `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` (PoseLandmarker Full).
- Tracks 33 body landmarks in both normalized 2D camera coordinates $(x, y)$
  and metric 3D camera space $(x, y, z)$.
- Real-time performance: Executes at 35–60 FPS inside the browser using WebGL
  acceleration.

4.2. 18-Point Anatomical Body Cage (`static/js/tracker.js`)
-----------------------------------------------------------
Raw skeleton landmarks only give joint pivot points (shoulders, elbows, hips).
Garments require surface geometry. `BodyTracker._buildBodyCage` constructs an
anatomical cage with 18 distinct landmarks:
1. `neck`: Midpoint between shoulder joints lifted toward the base of the skull.
2. `suprasternalNotch`: The hollow dip between the clavicles.
3. `clavicleLeft` & `clavicleRight`: Natural shoulder slope lines.
4. `leftShoulder` & `rightShoulder`: Acromion process tips.
5. `leftArmpit` & `rightArmpit`: Dynamic underarm junctions that elevate when
   arms raise.
6. `sternum`: Upper chest volume center ($0.70 \cdot \text{shoulder} + 0.30 \cdot \text{hip}$).
7. `solarPlexus`: Mid-torso spine anchor ($0.45 \cdot \text{shoulder} + 0.55 \cdot \text{hip}$).
8. `navel`: Lower abdomen / belt center ($0.20 \cdot \text{shoulder} + 0.80 \cdot \text{hip}$).
9. `leftRib` & `rightRib`: Lateral ribcage borders.
10. `leftWaist` & `rightWaist`: Natural narrowest waist indentations.
11. `leftHip` & `rightHip`: Pelvis lateral limits.
12. `hipCenter`: Sacrum / bottom hem center.
13. `leftElbow` & `rightElbow`: Upper arm vector terminators.
14. `leftWrist` & `rightWrist`: Forearm vector terminators.

4.3. Exponential Moving Average (EMA) Temporal Stabilization
------------------------------------------------------------
To eliminate high-frequency webcam landmark jitter without adding lag,
`tracker.js` implements dual-rate EMA smoothing:
$$P_{\text{smooth}}(t) = \alpha \cdot P_{\text{raw}}(t) + (1 - \alpha) \cdot P_{\text{smooth}}(t-1)$$
- Standard body joints: $\alpha = 0.50$ (smooth, organic stability).
- Rapid limb movements: Dynamically adapts $\alpha \to 0.85$ when joint velocity
  exceeds thresholds, ensuring zero latency during arm swings.

5. DUAL 3D TRY-ON DEFORMATION ENGINES (THREE.JS / WEBGL)
================================================================================
WearItNow features a dual-engine architecture accessible directly via top toggle
buttons (`[📐 Landmark Engine]` and `[🧬 SMPL Human Mesh]`):

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1. ENGINE 1: SMPL / HMR 2.0 PARAMETRIC HUMAN MESH RECOVERY (`static/js/smpl_model.js`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The SMPL engine represents the cutting edge of virtual try-on technology. Instead
of floating geometric panels, it constructs a complete 3D digital twin of the
user's body and binds the garment directly to this parametric surface.

A. 3D Digital Twin Parametric Human Mesh:
   - Generated dynamically with $32 \times 28$ vertices (896 vertices, 1,736
     faces).
   - Reconstructs a full 360-degree anatomical human surface: pelvis, lower
     abdomen, waist curve, pectoral dome, ribcage volume, back column, neck
     column, and shoulder caps.
   - Includes an optional holographic cyan wireframe cage (`[☑️ Body Twin]`) that
     tracks user motion in real time.

B. Surface-Bound Continuous 3D Garment Mesh:
   - A single unified topological cloth grid ($36 \times 36 = 1,369$ vertices,
     2,592 faces).
   - Unifies torso, shoulders, collar, and seamless sleeve caps into a SINGLE
     continuous mesh.
   - Solves the classic virtual try-on problem: ZERO mesh tearing, ZERO holes at
     the armpits, and ZERO floating seams.

C. Kinematic Frame Solving:
   Each frame, the solver computes an anatomical coordinate frame from 3D joints:
   - Acromion center: $C_{\text{midShoulder}} = \text{lerp}(C_{\text{ls}}, C_{\text{rs}}, 0.5)$
   - Spine Up-Vector ($v_{\text{up}}$): Unit vector pointing from solar plexus
     up to shoulder center.
   - Lateral Shoulder Vector ($u_{\text{shoulder}}$): Points from right to left shoulder.
   - Coronal Chest Normal ($n_{\text{chest}}$): $n_{\text{chest}} = v_{\text{up}} \times u_{\text{shoulder}}$.
     Represents the forward perpendicular vector pointing toward the camera (+Z).
   - Lateral Coronal Vector ($u_{\text{lat}}$): $u_{\text{lat}} = n_{\text{chest}} \times v_{\text{up}}$.

D. Armpit-Aligned Geometric Elevation:
   - Problem in naive meshes: Rows sag down in the center (e.g., center pinned to
     stomach while sides are at armpits), creating an unnatural chevron/V-dip.
   - Solution: At $v = 0.70$, the center spine anchor is set to $C_{\text{chestLevel}}$,
     which is mathematically projected at the exact same elevation along $v_{\text{up}}$
     as the armpits:
     $$\text{armpitElev} = (C_{\text{midArmpit}} - C_{\text{hipCenter}}) \cdot v_{\text{up}}$$
     $$C_{\text{chestLevel}} = C_{\text{hipCenter}} + v_{\text{up}} \cdot \text{armpitElev} + n_{\text{chest}} \cdot (0.035 \cdot sw)$$
   - Result: Every horizontal row across the torso is a true planar cross-section
     orthogonal to the spine.

E. Dynamic Planar UV Projection (Horizontal Stripe Anti-Warping):
   - Problem: If UVs are statically bound to grid indices, any physical deformation
     causes horizontal stripes on t-shirts to bend into chevron "V" shapes.
   - Solution: In `_solveAndDeform()`, the vertical texture coordinate $V_{\text{tex}}$
     is calculated dynamically for every vertex based on its absolute projection
     along the spine up-vector $v_{\text{up}}$:
     $$\text{vertexH} = (p - P_{\text{hemCenter}}) \cdot v_{\text{up}}$$
     $$\text{relH} = \text{clamp}\left(\frac{\text{vertexH}}{\text{totalTorsoH}}, 0.0, 1.05\right)$$
     $$V_{\text{torso}} = 0.11 + \text{relH} \cdot (0.86 - 0.11)$$
   - Mathematical guarantee: Any plane orthogonal to $v_{\text{up}}$ receives the
     EXACT same $V_{\text{tex}}$. Horizontal stripes on t-shirts remain strictly
     parallel and straight across the chest and stomach regardless of body movement!

F. Seamless Sleeve UV Unwrapping:
   - Torso width $|s| \le s_{\text{armhole}}$ maps across the central shirt
     region $[0.27, 0.73]$.
   - Sleeves map to the lateral image sleeve panels: Left sleeve maps to
     $[0.73, 0.93]$, Right sleeve maps to $[0.07, 0.27]$.
   - Continuous boundary blending at the armhole eliminates texture shearing.

G. 3D Flank Curvature & Ambient Occlusion Edge Darkening:
   - Flanks wrap backwards around the ribcage in true 3D depth:
     $$p \mathrel{+}= -n_{\text{chest}} \cdot \left(sw \cdot 0.075 \cdot \text{flankW}^{1.8}\right)$$
   - Vertex colors (`vertexColors: true` on `MeshStandardMaterial`) apply a subtle
     vignetted ambient occlusion roll-off: 100% brightness at the chest center,
     gently shading to 74% brightness at the lateral flanks, with a 12% under-hem
     shadow. This produces realistic cylindrical cloth lighting and depth.

H. Tailored Relaxed Drape:
   - Waist expansion scalar ($sw \cdot 0.080$) maintains a straight, athletic cut
     without pinching like a corset or flaring out like a boxy cylinder.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.2. ENGINE 2: LANDMARK DEFORMER ENGINE (`static/js/garment_models.js`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Landmark Engine provides maximum structural articulation for garments with
distinct, movable sleeves (jackets, hoodies, formal shirts):

A. Procedural Mesh Assembly:
   1. Torso Grid ($40 \times 40 = 1,681$ vertices, 3,200 faces).
   2. Left & Right Sleeve Tubes ($24 \times 24 = 625$ vertices each, 1,152 faces
      each) anchored at shoulder tips and draped along elbow-to-wrist vectors.
   3. Collar Ribbing Ring ($32 \times 2 = 66$ vertices).
   4. Hood Mesh (Hemispherical PBR cloth dome for hoodies).
   5. Forearm Occlusion Capsules (`colorWrite: false, depthWrite: true`).

B. Arm Elevation & Armhole Seam Welding:
   - Monitors elbow angle relative to shoulders (`leftArmLift`, `rightArmLift`).
   - When arms raise, the shoulder seam arches upward while the sleeve cylinder
     rim expands and rotates dynamically, maintaining seamless vertex alignment
     between sleeve and torso without tearing.

C. Silhouette-Aware Contour Edge Snapping:
   - Queries real-time silhouette bounds from the background segmenter.
   - Elastically snaps garment lateral borders ($L$ and $R$) to the user's
     detected outer body contours, eliminating overshooting or floating margins.

D. Invisible AR Forearm Occluders (Depth Masking):
   - Problem: In virtual try-on, virtual shirts render in front of the user's
     real forearms when they cross their arms across their chest.
   - Solution: Spawns invisible 3D cylinder capsules along the forearm vectors
     ($C_{\text{elbow}} \to C_{\text{wrist}}$) with `colorWrite: false` and
     `depthWrite: true`.
   - When the user lifts their arms across their body, the occluders write
     strictly to the WebGL Z-buffer. The GPU automatically occludes the virtual
     shirt, allowing the user's real hands and forearms to appear naturally in
     front of the clothing!

6. SCENE ORCHESTRATION & RENDERING CORE (`garment_renderer.js`, `app.js`)
================================================================================

6.1. Three.js PBR Cloth Studio Lighting & Projection Camera
-----------------------------------------------------------
- Camera: Perspective camera with dynamically updated aspect ratio matching the
  video feed dimensions ($1536 \times 826$, $1280 \times 720$, etc.).
- Realistic Studio Lighting Setup:
  * Ambient Light: Soft white base ambient (intensity: 1.25).
  * Key Light: Warm directional light (intensity: 1.35) positioned at (1.5, 2.5, 3.0).
  * Fill Light: Cool soft directional fill (intensity: 0.85) at (-2.0, 1.5, 2.0).
  * Rim Light: Subtle cyan rim light (intensity: 0.35) at (0, -1.0, 1.5) for edge
    separation.

6.2. Dual-Engine Switcher & Smooth Transform Interpolation
----------------------------------------------------------
- `garment_renderer.js` manages instant hot-swapping between `landmark` and `smpl`
  engines without reloading textures or dropping video frames.
- Smooth target interpolation: Positions, Euler rotations, and scales are
  linearly interpolated ($\text{LERP} = 0.25$) each frame to prevent jerky
  snapping when body tracking experiences noise.

6.3. Live Webcam vs. Calibrated Reference Subject Pipeline
----------------------------------------------------------
- Live Camera Mode: Connects to `navigator.mediaDevices.getUserMedia`, streaming
  live video into MediaPipe BlazePose for real-time try-on.
- Still Reference Subject Mode: Provides instant testing without a webcam using
  pre-packaged reference models (`Still: Male`, `Still: Female`, `Still: Desk`).
  Pre-calibrated ground-truth landmark cages allow automated verification.

7. CALIBRATION LAB & GROUND-TRUTH PRESETS (`calibrate.html`, `calibrate.js`)
================================================================================
WearItNow includes a dedicated engineering calibration suite accessible via the
`[📊 Calibration Lab]` button:
- 12+ Real-Time Fine-Tune Sliders:
  * Position (X, Y, Z offsets)
  * Scale (Uniform and independent Width / Height / Depth)
  * Rotation (Yaw, Pitch, Roll in degrees)
  * Arm Articulation Angles (Left / Right arm rotation)
  * Camera FOV & Distance calibration
- Ground-Truth Preset System:
  * Saves per-subject, per-garment calibrations to browser `localStorage` and
    exports formatted JSON code presets for `garment_renderer.js`.
  * Enables instant benchmark evaluation and unit testing across multiple body
    types.

9. INSTALLATION, SETUP, AND EXECUTION GUIDE
================================================================================

9.1. Prerequisites:
- Python 3.10 to 3.13 installed.
- Modern WebGL2-compatible browser (Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Firefox).
- Standard RGB webcam (optional; pre-loaded still reference subjects are included).

9.2. Installation Steps:
1. Open PowerShell or Command Prompt in the project folder:
   cd "p:\college\SEM 5\ARVR\CP\WearItNow"

2. Install Python dependencies:
   pip install -r requirements.txt

3. Start the WearItNow server:
   python run.py

4. Open your browser and navigate to:
   http://localhost:8000/index.html

9.3. Operational Instructions:
- Trying Sample Garments: Click any sample chip on the right panel (T-Shirt,
  Hoodie, Shirt, Jacket) to immediately project it on the subject.
- Uploading Custom Garments: Click the dashed upload box or drag-and-drop any
  garment image (PNG, JPG, WebP). The AI backend will classify, segment, and
  project it in under a second.
- Switching Deformation Engines:
  * Click `[📐 Landmark Engine]` for segmented multi-part deformation with
    forearm depth occluders.
  * Click `[🧬 SMPL Human Mesh]` for unified digital twin surface binding with
    dynamic planar UV horizontal stripe protection.
  * Toggle `[☑️ Body Twin]` to inspect the 3D parametric anatomical cage.
- Switching Camera Sources:
  * Click `[📷 Live Camera]` for real-time webcam virtual try-on.
  * Click `[🧍 Still: Male]`, `[👩 Still: Female]`, or `[💻 Still: Desk]` to test
    against ground-truth photo models.
- Calibration Lab: Click `[📊 Calibration Lab]` in the top header to enter the
  real-time fine-tune testing suite.
